Job Market Paper

Competitive Persuasion with a Noisy Judge
Latest version (PDF) SSRN

Can a firm that must select one of two competing agents benefit from judging their evidence with error, even though the error sometimes leads her to select a bad agent over a good one? I show that she can when the agents design their evidence themselves: noise weakens each agent's incentive to conceal, because a narrow lead over his rival no longer guarantees selection. A moderately noisy judge outperforms a perfectly accurate one unless agents are very likely to be bad, and attains the first best once they are more likely good than bad. For a broad class of noise distributions, the equilibrium is unique and simple: each agent concedes that he is bad with some probability and otherwise sends a single pooled signal. Within this class, the firm is always better off facing stronger agents, even though stronger agents may disclose less. Finally, the only selection rule that can deliver the first best for every prior leaves agents indifferent among all disclosures, whereas a firm that knows only whether the pool is strong or weak can make full disclosure the unique equilibrium.
