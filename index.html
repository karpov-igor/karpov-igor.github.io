Job Market Paper

Competitive Persuasion with a Noisy Judge
Latest version (PDF) SSRN

Can a firm choosing between two agents gain from judging them with error, even though noiseless judging is best for given evidence? I show that she can when the agents, each good or bad with the same prior, design their own evidence. Noiseless judging rewards even a narrow lead, so agents conceal bad news; suitably shaped noise devalues a narrow lead, so concealing pays less. For a broad class of noise, the equilibrium is unique: each agent sometimes concedes that he is bad and otherwise sends one pooled signal; for a high prior both disclose fully, which noiseless judging never induces. Moderate noise is best: it curbs concealment but never overturns a difference between the signals agents send. The best judge beats a noiseless one unless agents are very likely bad and attains the first best at priors above one half. Among rules of the signal gap, only a linear one attains the first best for every prior, leaving agents indifferent among all disclosures; knowing whether the pool is strong or weak suffices for a unique full disclosure equilibrium, though not under i.i.d. noise for weak pools.
