Job Market Paper

Competitive Persuasion with a Noisy Judge
Latest version (PDF) SSRN

Can a firm choosing between two agents gain from judging them with error? I show that she can when the agents design their own evidence. Noiseless judging makes even a narrow lead decisive, so agents conceal bad news not to lose; noise makes a narrow lead worth little, so revealing pays more. For a broad class of noise, if agents are either good or bad, the equilibrium is unique: each agent sometimes admits being bad and otherwise pools, and if agents are likely good, both reveal fully, unlike under noiseless judging. Moderate noise is best: it curbs concealment but never reverses the ranking of the signals sent. The best such judge beats a noiseless one unless agents are very likely bad, and attains the first best when they are at least as likely good as bad. With a continuum of types, the case for noise survives: even a designer who can measure evidence perfectly and commit to any rule prefers to judge with error for many priors.
